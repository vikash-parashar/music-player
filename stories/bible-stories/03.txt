Abraham and the Promise of the Stars

Long ago, in the ancient land between two great rivers, there lived a man named Abram. He was not a king or a warrior. He was a herdsman, with flocks of sheep and goats, and tents, and a large household. His wife was named Sarai, and she was known for her beauty. But for all their blessings, there was one deep sorrow in their lives. They had no children. And as the years went by, they grew old, and the hope of a child slowly faded.

Then one day, when Abram was already seventy five years old, God spoke to him.

Get thee out of thy country, God said, and from thy kindred, and from thy father's house, unto a land that I will shew thee.

It was an extraordinary request. God was asking Abram to pack up everything he owned, leave his home and his family, and set out for a place he had never seen. God did not even tell him where it was. But God also made him a promise. I will make of thee a great nation, God said, and I will bless thee, and make thy name great. And in thee shall all families of the earth be blessed.

A great nation. From a man who had no children at all.

Most people would have stayed where they were. But Abram believed God. So he gathered up Sarai, and his nephew Lot, and all their servants and animals, and he set out on the long road west. Day after day they travelled, across dry hills and dusty plains, until at last they came to the land of Canaan. And there God appeared to him again and said, Unto thy seed will I give this land.

Abram built an altar there and worshipped. And then he kept on travelling, living in tents, moving from place to place with his flocks.

Years passed. Still there was no child. One night, Abram was feeling discouraged. He spoke to God honestly about it. Lord, he said, what good are all these gifts, when I have no son? A servant in my house will inherit everything I have.

And God answered him in a way he would never forget. He brought Abram outside, under the open sky. It was a clear desert night, far from any city lights, and the heavens were blazing with stars. Thousands upon thousands of them, from one edge of the sky to the other.

Look now toward heaven, God said, and tell the stars, if thou be able to number them. So shall thy seed be.

Abram looked up. He could not count them. No one could. And standing there in the cool night air, an old man with no children, he believed the promise of God. The Bible says that God counted it to him for righteousness.

More years went by. God gave Abram a new name. No longer Abram, but Abraham, which means father of many nations. And Sarai became Sarah, which means princess.

Then one hot afternoon, when Abraham was ninety nine years old, he was sitting at the door of his tent when he looked up and saw three visitors standing nearby. Abraham jumped up and ran to greet them. He bowed low and begged them to stay and rest. He had water brought to wash their feet, and he hurried to Sarah and asked her to bake fresh bread. He chose a tender calf and had it prepared, and he served them a feast under the shade of a tree.

As they ate, one of the visitors asked, Where is Sarah thy wife?

She is in the tent, Abraham answered.

And the visitor said that by this time next year, Sarah would have a son.

Now Sarah was standing just inside the tent door, listening. And when she heard this, she laughed to herself. She was ninety years old. How could she possibly have a baby now?

But the visitor heard her. Why did Sarah laugh, he asked. Is any thing too hard for the Lord?

It is one of the great questions of the Bible. Is any thing too hard for the Lord?

And sure enough, just as God had promised, Sarah gave birth to a baby boy. Abraham was a hundred years old. They named their son Isaac, which means laughter. And Sarah said, God hath made me to laugh, so that all that hear will laugh with me. Her laugh of doubt had become a laugh of pure joy.

Isaac grew up, and in time he had sons of his own, twins named Esau and Jacob. And Jacob had twelve sons, and from those sons came the twelve tribes of a great people, just as God had promised. More than the stars in the sky.

But the road from that promise to its fulfillment was not always smooth. Among Jacob's twelve sons, there was one boy his father loved more than all the rest. A boy who wore a coat of many colors, and who dreamed strange dreams. His name was Joseph.
