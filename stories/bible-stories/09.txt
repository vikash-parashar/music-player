Jonah and the Great Fish

There was once a prophet named Jonah, the son of Amittai. One day, the word of the Lord came to him with a task he did not want.

Arise, God said, go to Nineveh, that great city, and cry against it. For their wickedness is come up before me.

Nineveh was the capital of Assyria, a powerful and fearsome empire, and an enemy of Jonah's people. And the people there had done many cruel things.

Jonah did not want to go. Perhaps he was afraid. Or perhaps, deep down, he did not want the people of Nineveh to be given a second chance. So instead of heading east toward Nineveh, Jonah went down to the port of Joppa and found a ship sailing west, to a faraway place called Tarshish. He paid his fare and climbed aboard. He was trying, the Bible says, to flee from the presence of the Lord.

Jonah went below deck, lay down, and fell fast asleep.

But you cannot run away from God. Out on the open sea, the Lord sent a great wind. The sky turned black. Waves rose up like mountains and crashed over the deck. The ship rocked and groaned until it seemed it would break apart.

The sailors were terrified. Every man cried out to his own god for help. They threw the cargo overboard to make the ship lighter. And still the storm raged on.

Then the captain went below and found Jonah, still sleeping. What meanest thou, O sleeper, he shouted. Arise, call upon thy God. Perhaps He will think upon us, that we perish not.

The sailors cast lots to find out who was to blame for this storm. And the lot fell on Jonah.

Jonah told them the truth. I am a Hebrew, he said, and I fear the Lord, the God of heaven, which hath made the sea and the dry land. And he told them he was running away from God.

The sailors were even more afraid. What shall we do unto thee, they asked, that the sea may be calm?

Jonah answered, Take me up, and cast me forth into the sea. So shall the sea be calm unto you. For I know that for my sake this great tempest is upon you.

The sailors did not want to do it. They rowed hard, trying with all their might to bring the ship back to land. But the sea only grew wilder. At last, they prayed to the Lord for mercy, and they lifted Jonah up and threw him over the side.

At once, the sea grew calm. And the sailors stood on the deck in wonder, and they worshipped the Lord.

As for Jonah, he sank down into the cold, dark water. It seemed certain that he would drown.

But the Lord had prepared a great fish to swallow up Jonah.

Out of the deep came an enormous creature. Its mouth opened wide, and Jonah was swallowed whole. And there, in the belly of the fish, Jonah was alive.

He was in the belly of the fish three days and three nights. And there, in that unlikely place, Jonah began to pray. He cried out to God from the depths, and he thanked God for saving his life. He promised to do what God asked. And he ended his prayer with these words. Salvation is of the Lord.

Then God spoke to the fish. And the fish swam to the shore and cast Jonah out onto the dry land.

Once more the word of the Lord came to Jonah. Arise, go unto Nineveh.

This time, Jonah went.

He walked into that great city and began to cry out. Yet forty days, and Nineveh shall be overthrown.

And something amazing happened. The people of Nineveh believed God. They stopped what they were doing and put on rough sackcloth as a sign of sorrow. From the greatest to the least, they turned from their wicked ways. Even the king rose from his throne, laid aside his royal robes, and sat in ashes. Who can tell, the king said, if God will turn and repent, and turn away from his fierce anger, that we perish not?

And when God saw that they had truly changed, He had mercy on them. The city was spared.

But Jonah was not happy. He sat outside the city, sulking in the hot sun. I knew it, he said to God. I knew that thou art a gracious God, and merciful, slow to anger, and of great kindness.

So God caused a leafy vine to grow up overnight and give Jonah shade. Jonah was glad of it. But the next day, a worm attacked the vine and it withered away. And Jonah was angry about the plant.

Then God spoke gently to him. You cared about a plant that grew up in a single night. Should I not care about Nineveh, that great city, with more than a hundred and twenty thousand people, and much cattle besides?

God's mercy was bigger than Jonah had imagined. And in the court of Persia, in a later age, God's care for His people would be shown through a young woman who became a queen. Her name was Esther.
