David and Goliath

In the hills outside the little town of Bethlehem, a young shepherd boy sat among his father's sheep. His name was David. He was the youngest of eight brothers, with ruddy cheeks and bright eyes. While the sheep grazed, David played his harp and sang songs to God. The Lord is my shepherd, he would write, I shall not want.

But being a shepherd was not always peaceful. Once, a lion crept out of the brush and snatched a lamb from the flock. David chased after it and rescued the lamb from its mouth. Another time, a bear attacked, and David fought it off too. He knew he had not done it by his own strength alone. God had been with him.

At that time, Israel had a king named Saul. And Israel had enemies, the Philistines, who lived along the coast. One day, the armies of the Philistines and the armies of Israel gathered for battle. The Philistines stood on a mountain on one side, and Israel stood on a mountain on the other side, and between them lay the valley of Elah.

Every morning, a man came out from the camp of the Philistines and stood in that valley. His name was Goliath, from the city of Gath. And he was a giant. The Bible says he stood six cubits and a span, which is well over nine feet tall. He wore a bronze helmet and a coat of armor so heavy that a normal man could barely lift it. The shaft of his spear was like a weaver's beam, and its iron point alone weighed more than a small child.

Goliath's voice boomed across the valley. Choose a man to fight me, he roared. If he wins, we will be your servants. But if I win, you will be our servants. I defy the armies of Israel this day. Give me a man, that we may fight together.

When Saul and his soldiers heard this, they were dismayed, and greatly afraid. No one stepped forward. For forty days, morning and evening, Goliath came out and shouted his challenge. And for forty days, no one answered.

Now three of David's older brothers were in Saul's army. One day, David's father Jesse sent him to the camp with bread and cheese for his brothers. David arrived just as Goliath strode out to shout his challenge again. And David saw the soldiers of Israel run away in fear.

David could not believe it. Who is this Philistine, he asked, that he should defy the armies of the living God?

His oldest brother Eliab heard him and was angry. Why did you come down here, he said. But David would not be quiet. And soon his words reached King Saul, who sent for him.

David stood before the king. Let no man's heart fail because of him, he said. Thy servant will go and fight with this Philistine.

Saul looked at him. Thou art but a youth, he said, and he a man of war.

But David told Saul about the lion and the bear. The Lord that delivered me out of the paw of the lion, and out of the paw of the bear, he will deliver me out of the hand of this Philistine.

Saul was moved. Go, he said, and the Lord be with thee.

Saul dressed David in his own armor, with a bronze helmet and a heavy coat of mail. He buckled his own sword to David's side. But when David tried to walk, he could hardly move. I cannot go with these, he said, for I have not proved them. So he took it all off.

Instead, he picked up his shepherd's staff. He went down to a stream and chose five smooth stones, and put them in his shepherd's bag. And with his sling in his hand, he walked out into the valley.

When Goliath saw him coming, a boy with a stick and a sling, he was insulted. Am I a dog, he bellowed, that thou comest to me with staves? Come to me, and I will give thy flesh to the birds of the air.

David did not slow down. He called back across the valley, and his voice rang clear. Thou comest to me with a sword, and with a spear, and with a shield. But I come to thee in the name of the Lord of hosts. This day the Lord will deliver thee into mine hand, that all the earth may know that there is a God in Israel. For the battle is the Lord's.

Goliath lumbered forward. And David ran toward him.

He reached into his bag, took out a single stone, placed it in his sling, and whirled it around. Then he let it fly. The stone sailed through the air and struck the giant right in the forehead. And Goliath fell face down on the ground.

A great shout went up from the armies of Israel. The Philistines turned and fled. The shepherd boy had won.

David grew up to become the king of Israel, and Jerusalem became his city. And centuries later, in the faraway city of Babylon, another young man from Israel would face a danger just as fierce. His name was Daniel.
