Joseph and the Coat of Many Colors

Jacob had twelve sons, but there was no hiding it. Joseph was his favorite. Joseph was the son of Rachel, the wife Jacob loved most, and he had been born when Jacob was already old. To show his love, Jacob gave Joseph a special gift. A coat of many colors. It was beautiful, the kind of garment a prince might wear.

His older brothers saw that coat, and they saw how their father looked at Joseph. And the Bible says they hated him, and could not speak peaceably unto him.

It did not help that Joseph had dreams. One morning, he told his brothers about one of them. We were all out in the field, he said, tying up bundles of grain. Suddenly my bundle stood up straight, and all your bundles gathered around and bowed down to mine.

His brothers stared at him. Shalt thou indeed reign over us, they asked.

Then Joseph had another dream. This time, he said, the sun and the moon and eleven stars all bowed down to me.

One day, Jacob sent Joseph out to check on his brothers, who were far away tending the flocks. When the brothers saw him coming in the distance, in that bright coat, their anger boiled over. Behold, this dreamer cometh, they said. Let us get rid of him, and we shall see what will become of his dreams.

The eldest brother, Reuben, would not let them kill him. Throw him into this pit instead, he said, secretly planning to rescue Joseph later. So when Joseph arrived, they grabbed him, tore off his coat of many colors, and threw him into a dry, empty pit.

While Reuben was away, a caravan of traders came by, heading for Egypt with camels loaded with spices. Another brother, Judah, had an idea. Why not sell Joseph to them? And so they did, for twenty pieces of silver. The traders carried Joseph away, and his brothers watched him disappear down the road to Egypt.

Then they dipped his coat in goat's blood and brought it home to their father. When Jacob saw it, he wept and tore his clothes. A wild beast has devoured him, he cried. And he would not be comforted.

But Joseph was alive. In Egypt he was sold as a servant to an officer named Potiphar. And the Bible says, the Lord was with Joseph. Everything he did turned out well, and soon Potiphar put him in charge of his whole house. But Potiphar's wife told lies about Joseph, and he was thrown into prison for something he did not do.

Even in prison, God was with him. Two of them, the king's butler and the king's baker, had strange dreams one night. Joseph explained what their dreams meant, and everything happened exactly as he said. The butler was set free. Remember me, Joseph begged him. But the butler forgot.

Two whole years went by. Then one night, Pharaoh, the king of Egypt, had a troubling dream. He saw seven fat, healthy cows come up out of the river Nile. Then seven thin, starving cows came up after them, and ate the fat ones. None of Pharaoh's wise men could tell him what it meant.

Suddenly the butler remembered. There is a young man in prison, he told Pharaoh, who can explain dreams.

It is not in me, Joseph said humbly. God shall give Pharaoh an answer.

Then he explained. The seven fat cows were seven years of plenty. The seven thin cows were seven years of famine that would follow. Egypt must store up grain during the good years, so the people would not starve in the bad years.

Pharaoh was amazed. He made Joseph ruler over all of Egypt, second only to the king himself. Joseph was thirty years old.

Everything happened just as Joseph had said. Seven years of plenty, then a terrible famine that spread to every land. Far away in Canaan, Jacob's family began to go hungry. So Jacob sent ten of his sons down to Egypt to buy grain.

They came before the governor of Egypt and bowed low, with their faces to the ground. They did not recognize him. But Joseph recognized them. His dream had come true.

Joseph tested his brothers to see if their hearts had changed. And they had. When Joseph threatened to keep the youngest brother, Benjamin, as a servant, Judah stepped forward and begged to take Benjamin's place, so that their old father would not lose another son.

Joseph could not hold back any longer. I am Joseph, he said. Doth my father yet live?

His brothers were terrified. But Joseph said, Do not be grieved or angry with yourselves. God sent me before you to save lives. Ye thought evil against me, but God meant it unto good.

So Jacob and his whole family came to live in Egypt, and the old man held his lost son once more.

They stayed, and grew, and became a great people. But long after Joseph was gone, a new Pharaoh rose up who did not remember him. And for the children of Israel, hard times were coming.
