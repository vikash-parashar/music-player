Daniel in the Lions' Den

Long after the days of King David, the city of Jerusalem was conquered by the mighty empire of Babylon. Many of its people were carried far away into exile. Among them was a young man named Daniel.

Daniel was taken to the royal court of Babylon, where he was trained to serve the king. But in his heart, he stayed faithful to the God of his fathers. Even as a young man, Daniel purposed in his heart that he would not turn away from the ways of his God.

Daniel served faithfully through the reigns of several kings. He was wise and honest, and God gave him the gift of understanding dreams and visions. The years went by, and Babylon itself fell to a new power, the kingdom of the Medes and the Persians. A new king, Darius, came to the throne.

By now Daniel was an old man, perhaps more than eighty years old. And King Darius thought very highly of him. The king set one hundred and twenty princes over the kingdom, with three presidents above them. And Daniel was the first of those three. In fact, because an excellent spirit was in him, the king was thinking of putting Daniel in charge of the whole kingdom.

But the other presidents and princes were jealous. Who was this foreigner, this exile, to be set over them? So they began to watch Daniel closely, hoping to catch him doing something wrong.

They watched and watched. But they found nothing. Daniel was faithful in everything.

At last they said to one another, We shall not find any occasion against this Daniel, except we find it concerning the law of his God.

So they came up with a clever plan. They went to King Darius together and flattered him. O king, live for ever, they said. All the leaders of the kingdom have agreed that you should make a new law. For thirty days, no one may pray to any god or man except to you, O king. And anyone who breaks this law shall be thrown into the den of lions.

Darius was pleased by the idea. And so he signed the decree. Now, according to the law of the Medes and Persians, once the king signed a law, it could never be changed.

Daniel soon heard about the new law. And what did he do?

He went home. He climbed the stairs to his upper room, where the windows opened toward Jerusalem, far away across the desert. And there he knelt down, just as he always did, three times a day, and prayed and gave thanks before his God, as he had done before.

He did not hide. He did not close the windows. He simply kept being faithful.

The jealous men were watching. They burst in and found Daniel on his knees, praying to God. And they hurried straight to the king.

Did you not sign a law, they asked Darius, that anyone who prays to any god but you for thirty days shall be thrown to the lions?

The thing is true, said the king.

That Daniel, they said, regards not thee, O king. He makes his petition three times a day.

When Darius heard this, he was sore displeased with himself. He loved Daniel. He realized now that he had been tricked. All day long, until the sun went down, he tried to find a way to save his friend. But the men reminded him that the law of the Medes and Persians could not be changed.

So, with a heavy heart, the king gave the order. Daniel was brought and cast into the den of lions. And as he went down into the dark, the king called out to him. Thy God whom thou servest continually, he will deliver thee.

A great stone was rolled over the mouth of the den. The king sealed it with his own ring, so no one could move it.

Then Darius went back to his palace. And all night long, he could not sleep.

At the very first light of morning, the king rose and hurried to the den. He called out in a voice full of sorrow. O Daniel, servant of the living God, is thy God, whom thou servest continually, able to deliver thee from the lions?

For a moment, there was silence. And then a calm voice answered from the darkness below.

O king, live for ever. My God hath sent his angel, and hath shut the lions' mouths, that they have not hurt me.

The king was overjoyed. He commanded that Daniel be lifted up out of the den. And when they brought him out, there was not a scratch on him, because he believed in his God.

Then King Darius wrote to all the peoples of his kingdom, declaring that the God of Daniel is the living God, whose kingdom shall never be destroyed. And Daniel prospered for the rest of his days.

Daniel had stayed faithful when it was dangerous. But not everyone in the Bible was so quick to do what God asked. There was once a prophet who, when God told him to go east, got on a ship and sailed the other way. His name was Jonah.
