Moses and the Burning Bush

Many years after Joseph died, his family had grown into a great multitude. The children of Israel filled the land of Goshen in Egypt. But a new Pharaoh had come to the throne, one who knew nothing of Joseph and all he had done to save Egypt. This Pharaoh looked at the Israelites and felt afraid. There were so many of them. What if they turned against him?

So he made them slaves. He set cruel taskmasters over them and forced them to make bricks and build great cities.

Then Pharaoh gave a terrible order. Every baby boy born to the Hebrews was to be thrown into the river.

During those dark days, a Hebrew woman named Jochebed gave birth to a son. He was a beautiful baby, and she could not bear to give him up.

So she made a little basket out of bulrushes, the tall reeds that grew by the river. Then she laid her baby inside, and set the basket gently among the reeds at the edge of the Nile. His big sister Miriam hid nearby to watch what would happen.

Soon, Pharaoh's own daughter came down to the river to bathe. She saw the basket among the reeds and sent her maid to fetch it. When she opened it, the baby was crying. And her heart was moved with compassion. This is one of the Hebrews' children, she said.

Just then, Miriam stepped out bravely. Shall I go and find a Hebrew woman to nurse the baby for you, she asked. The princess said yes. And so Miriam ran and fetched her own mother. The baby was given back to the very woman who had made the basket, and she was even paid to care for him.

When the boy was older, he went to live in the palace as the son of Pharaoh's daughter. She named him Moses, because, she said, I drew him out of the water.

Moses grew up as an Egyptian prince. But he never forgot who his people were. One day, when he was grown, he saw an Egyptian beating a Hebrew slave. In his anger, Moses struck the Egyptian and killed him. When Pharaoh heard of it, he sought to kill Moses. So Moses fled far away, into the land of Midian.

There, Moses began a new and quiet life. He married a woman named Zipporah and became a shepherd, caring for the flocks of her father, Jethro.

Meanwhile, back in Egypt, the children of Israel cried out to God in their suffering. And God heard their groaning, and remembered His promise to Abraham, to Isaac, and to Jacob.

One day, Moses led his flock far into the desert, to the foot of a mountain called Horeb, the mountain of God. And there he saw something strange.

A bush was on fire. Flames leaped from its branches. But the bush was not burning up.

Moses stopped. I will now turn aside, he said, and see this great sight, why the bush is not burnt.

As he came closer, a voice called to him from the middle of the bush. Moses, Moses.

And Moses answered, Here am I.

Draw not nigh hither, the voice said. Put off thy shoes from off thy feet, for the place whereon thou standest is holy ground.

Moses slipped off his sandals. Then God spoke again. I am the God of thy father, the God of Abraham, the God of Isaac, and the God of Jacob. And Moses hid his face, for he was afraid to look upon God.

God told Moses that He had seen the suffering of His people. He had heard their cries. And now He was going to rescue them, and bring them out of Egypt into a good land, a land flowing with milk and honey. And Moses was the one He would send.

But Moses was not so sure. Who am I, he asked, that I should go unto Pharaoh?

Certainly I will be with thee, God replied.

But what if the people ask me your name, Moses said. What shall I tell them?

And God gave an answer full of mystery. I AM THAT I AM. Tell them, I AM hath sent me unto you.

Still Moses was afraid. What if they do not believe me? So God gave him signs. He told Moses to throw his shepherd's staff on the ground, and it became a snake. When Moses picked it up by the tail, it turned back into a staff.

But Moses still had one more worry. I am slow of speech, he said. I am not good with words.

Who hath made man's mouth, God asked gently. Now go, and I will be with thy mouth, and teach thee what thou shalt say. And God told him that his brother Aaron would go with him and help him speak.

So Moses went back to Jethro, gathered his wife and sons, and took the staff of God in his hand. The shepherd turned toward Egypt. He was going to stand before the most powerful king on earth and say four words that would change history. Let my people go.
