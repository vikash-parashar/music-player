Noah and the Great Flood

Many generations after Adam and Eve left the garden, the world had grown crowded and noisy. But it had also grown cruel. People had forgotten the God who made them. They lied and stole and fought. The old words say that the earth was filled with violence, and that every imagination of the thoughts of the human heart was only evil continually.

God looked upon all of this, and it grieved Him to His heart.

But in the middle of all that darkness, there was one small light. His name was Noah. Noah was a just man, the Bible says, and he walked with God. He had a wife and three sons, Shem, Ham, and Japheth, and each of his sons had a wife of his own. They were an ordinary family in most ways. But Noah listened when God spoke.

One day, God told Noah something astonishing. A great flood was coming, a flood that would cover the whole earth. And Noah was to build an ark, a huge wooden boat, to save his family and the animals of the world.

God gave him the plans. The ark was to be made of gopher wood and sealed inside and out with pitch so no water could get in. It was to be three hundred cubits long, fifty cubits wide, and thirty cubits high. That is longer than a football field and taller than a four story building. It would have three decks, one window near the top, and a single great door in its side.

Now picture Noah standing on dry land, far from any sea, beginning to build a boat the size of a palace. His neighbors must have laughed. But Noah kept working. Day after day, year after year, he cut the timber and fitted the beams. The Bible says simply that Noah did according to all that God commanded him.

At last, the ark was finished. And then something wonderful happened. The animals began to arrive.

They came from the forests and the plains, from the hills and the deserts. Two by two they came, a male and a female of every kind. Lions padded up the ramp beside zebras. Rabbits hopped next to wolves. Eagles swooped down and settled in the rafters. Tiny mice scurried between the feet of elephants. Of the clean animals and birds, seven pairs came, so there would be enough when the flood was over. Noah and his family had gathered food for all of them, sacks and baskets and barrels of it.

When everyone was safely inside, Noah, his wife, his sons, and their wives went in too. And then, the Bible says, the Lord shut them in.

Seven days passed. Then the sky grew dark.

It began to rain. Not a gentle shower, but a pounding, roaring rain. The fountains of the great deep burst open, and the windows of heaven were opened. Water rushed up from below and poured down from above. Rivers overflowed. Valleys filled. The ark creaked and groaned, and then slowly, gently, it lifted off the ground and began to float.

For forty days and forty nights the rain fell. The water rose higher and higher until even the tallest mountains were covered. Everything outside the ark was gone. But inside, in the warm dark belly of that great boat, Noah and his family and all the animals were safe.

And then, the Bible says, God remembered Noah.

He sent a wind across the earth, and the rain stopped. Slowly, the waters began to go down. After many months, the ark came to rest on the mountains of Ararat. But Noah could not yet see whether the land was dry.

So he opened the window and sent out a raven. The raven flew back and forth, back and forth, finding nowhere to land. Then Noah sent out a dove. But the dove found no place to rest its feet, and it came back to him. Noah reached out his hand and gently brought it inside.

He waited seven more days and sent the dove out again. This time, in the evening, the dove returned with something in its beak. A fresh olive leaf, plucked off. Somewhere out there, trees were growing again.

Noah waited seven more days and sent the dove out one last time. And this time, it did not come back. It had found a home.

At last God said to Noah, Go forth of the ark. And out they came, Noah and his family and every living creature, stepping blinking into the sunshine of a washed and shining world.

The first thing Noah did was build an altar and give thanks to God. And God made a promise. Never again would He send a flood to destroy the earth. As a sign of that promise, He set something in the clouds. A rainbow, arching across the sky in every color of the light.

So whenever you see a rainbow after the rain, you can remember that promise.

Noah's sons had children, and their children spread across the earth. And many generations later, in a city called Ur, there lived a man whom God would call to leave everything behind. His name was Abram.
