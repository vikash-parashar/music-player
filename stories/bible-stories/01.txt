In the Beginning: Creation and the Garden

Before there were mountains or oceans, before there was a single star in the sky, there was only darkness. The old words of Genesis put it simply. In the beginning God created the heaven and the earth. And the earth was without form, and void, and darkness was upon the face of the deep.

Moving over that water, gently, like a bird hovering over its nest, was the Spirit of God.

Then God spoke. And the very first thing He said was this. Let there be light.

And there was light.

It poured across the waters, warm and golden, and God saw that the light was good. He divided the light from the darkness. The light He called Day, and the darkness He called Night. And the evening and the morning were the first day.

On the second day, God stretched out the sky like a great blue canopy, separating the waters above from the waters below. On the third day, He gathered the seas together into one place, and dry land rose up out of the deep. And then, almost at once, that bare land burst into green. Grass spread across the hills. Trees pushed up toward the sky, heavy with fruit. Flowers opened their faces, and every plant carried seeds inside it, ready to grow again and again. And God saw that it was good.

On the fourth day, God hung lights in the heavens. He set the great sun to rule the day and the gentle moon to rule the night, and He scattered the stars across the darkness like a handful of silver dust.

On the fifth day, the waters came alive. Fish darted through the shallows. Great whales rolled in the deep. And above the water, birds of every color took to the air, singing as they flew. God blessed them all and told them to be fruitful and fill the earth.

On the sixth day, the land itself was filled with creatures. Every kind of animal, large and small, walked and crept and galloped across the new world.

And then God did something different. He knelt, so to speak, in the dust of the ground, and He formed a man. He breathed into him the breath of life, and the man became a living soul. His name was Adam. And God made him in His own image, to care for the earth and everything in it.

God planted a garden for Adam eastward in Eden. It was the most beautiful place that has ever been. A river flowed through it to water every tree, and the trees were pleasant to look at and good for food. In the middle of the garden stood two special trees. One was the tree of life. The other was the tree of the knowledge of good and evil.

God told Adam he could eat freely from every tree in the garden except one. Of the tree of the knowledge of good and evil, thou shalt not eat of it.

Then God brought the animals to Adam, one by one, to see what he would call them. And whatever Adam called each creature, that was its name. But among all those animals, there was no one quite like Adam, no true companion for him. So God caused a deep sleep to fall upon him, and from his side God made a woman. When Adam woke and saw her, his heart was glad. Her name would be Eve, and together they walked in the garden, at peace with God and with each other.

On the seventh day, God rested from all His work. He blessed that day and made it holy.

For a time, everything was perfect. But one day a serpent came to Eve. It was more subtle, more cunning, than any other creature. And it asked her a question that planted a seed of doubt. Did God really say you could not eat from every tree? The serpent told her the forbidden fruit would open her eyes and make her wise like God.

Eve looked at the tree. The fruit was lovely, and she wanted the wisdom it promised. So she took some and ate it, and she gave some to Adam, and he ate too.

At once, everything changed. They felt ashamed and afraid. When they heard God walking in the garden in the cool of the day, they hid among the trees.

God called out, Where art thou?

It is a question full of sorrow, but also full of love. God did not stop looking for them. Though Adam and Eve had to leave the garden, God made coats of skins to clothe them before they went. Even in that sad moment, He was still caring for them.

Outside the garden, life was harder. There was work and weariness, thorns and thistles. But there was also hope. Adam and Eve had children, and their children had children, and the world slowly filled with people.

Yet as the years rolled on, the hearts of people grew darker and darker. Kindness faded, and violence spread across the land. And God looked down with grief, searching for even one person who still walked with Him.

He found one. A man named Noah.
